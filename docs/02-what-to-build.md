# 2. What to ask Fable to build

Rule for every idea: **one small thing, for one clear type of person, that solves one annoying problem.**

## Idea 1 - Websites for local businesses (start here)

**Who pays:** barbers, salons, restaurants, tutors, mechanics, tailors, churches, small schools. Many have only a Facebook page or nothing.

**What Fable builds:** a one-page site with name, photos, services and prices, opening hours, map link and a big "Chat on WhatsApp" button.

**How you earn:** a one-time setup fee, plus a small monthly fee for updates if they agree. Set the price to what is normal in your area; start low to get your first three examples, then raise it.

**Why it works with $0:** hosting is free on GitHub Pages, and the client is someone you can visit in person.

**Steps:**
1. Pick one business type (for example barbers).
2. Ask Fable for a demo site for an invented barber (prompt in `prompts/01-local-business-site.md`).
3. Publish it. This is your sample.
4. Show the sample on your phone to 10 real barbers. Offer: "I make this with your name and photos by tomorrow. Pay only if you like it."
5. For each yes: collect name, prices, photos via WhatsApp, ask Fable to swap them in, publish, get paid.

## Idea 2 - Digital templates

**Who pays:** students, job seekers, freelancers, small shop owners.

**What Fable builds:** CV templates, invoice and receipt templates, monthly budget planners, study timetables, business plan outlines, social media caption packs - as printable HTML/PDF or Google Sheets/Docs layouts.

**How you earn:** list each for a small price or pay-what-you-want on Gumroad/Ko-fi/Selar. Bundles sell better than single files.

**Steps:**
1. Choose one audience (for example "final-year students applying for jobs").
2. Ask Fable for 5 templates for that audience (prompt in `prompts/02-digital-templates.md`).
3. Open each on your phone, use Chrome **Share > Print > Save as PDF**.
4. Upload the PDFs as one product. Ask Fable to write the product description.
5. Share where that audience already is: class WhatsApp groups, Facebook groups, TikTok/Reels showing the template being filled in.

## Idea 3 - Micro web tools

**What Fable builds:** single-page tools - loan/savings calculator, exam grade (GPA) calculator, unit price comparer for market shopping, data bundle cost comparer, invoice generator, QR code maker, tip splitter.

**How you earn:** a "Support me" tip link, relevant affiliate links, and ads only after you have steady visitors. This is slow; treat it as a long-term asset and as a portfolio that proves your skill for Idea 1.

A working example is in `examples/invoice-generator/`.

## Idea 4 - WhatsApp business helpers

**Who pays:** small shops and food sellers who take orders by chat.

**What Fable builds:** a menu or price-list page where the customer taps items and the page opens WhatsApp with the order already typed. No server needed.

**How you earn:** setup fee per shop. Easy to sell because the owner sees fewer back-and-forth messages on day one.

A working example is in `examples/whatsapp-order-page/`.

## Idea 5 - Small browser games

**What Fable builds:** simple one-file games (quiz, memory, word puzzle, endless runner). Publish on Itch.io as pay-what-you-want.

**Honest note:** very few small games earn meaningful money. Do this only if you enjoy it.

## Idea 6 - Niche content site

**What Fable builds:** a simple site of helpful guides on something you actually know (exam prep for your country, phone repair tips, farming, local recipes), ideally in your local language where there is less competition.

**How you earn:** affiliate links and ads once traffic exists. Expect months, not weeks. Check every fact yourself; do not publish unreviewed AI text.

## What NOT to ask for

- Native Android apps for the Play Store as a first step: the developer account costs money and building APKs without a PC is painful. Use web pages instead; they can be "installed" from Chrome with **Add to Home screen**.
- Betting predictors, trading bots, "earn by watching ads" apps, fake review or follower tools.
- Copies of someone else's brand, app or content.
